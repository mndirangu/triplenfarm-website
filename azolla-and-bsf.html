Circular feed systems

Using azolla and black soldier fly responsibly

Local feed resources can reduce waste and diversify ingredients—but they still require careful ration design.

Azolla and black soldier fly larvae are often promoted as low-cost feed solutions. Both can be useful, but neither should be treated as a complete replacement for balanced feed.

Azolla: useful but variable

Fresh azolla contains substantial water, and its nutrient content varies with cultivation conditions. Introduce it gradually, maintain clean production water and monitor animal acceptance and performance.

Black soldier fly larvae: converting suitable organic material

BSF larvae can convert selected organic materials into a useful protein and fat ingredient. The safety of the substrate matters. Avoid contaminated, chemical-laden or unsafe waste streams.

Balance the full ration

Animals require energy, protein, amino acids, minerals and vitamins in the right proportions. Farm-grown inputs should form part of a calculated ration rather than being added without limits.

Practical rule: introduce one change at a time, record feed intake and animal performance, and consult a qualified nutrition or animal-health professional when formulating commercial-scale rations.
